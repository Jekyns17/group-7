THE WRITING CRAFT • AUTHOR'S PERSPECTIVE

Crafting Worlds on Paper: The Discipline of Writing Fiction at University

By Daniella Evelyn • Makerere University, Kampala

To write a novel is to construct an entire reality from silence. While my university coursework challenges analytical reasoning, writing fiction is where I breathe life into characters, explore moral ambiguities, and capture the subtleties of human experience.

Writing books is not a hobby reserved for spare hours; it is a dedicated discipline. Every morning before lectures begin, sitting down with a blank page and purple fountain pen allows me to translate fleeting observations of campus life into layered narratives.

Great literature teaches empathy. When you author a story, you must understand every protagonist, antagonist, and bystander from within. It demands relentless rewriting, honest self-critique, and patience.

"You can make anything by writing. A blank page is the ultimate canvas of human freedom."

Contributing to our Group 7 project has shown me that storytelling and technical execution share the same root: structuring complex ideas so clearly that anyone can step into the world you built.
